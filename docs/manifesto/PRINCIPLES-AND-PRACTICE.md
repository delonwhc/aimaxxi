# AI Maximalism: Principles and Practice

*Draft v1 — September 27, 2026. Unpublished writing for review.*

AI/MAXXI is the public expression of the philosophy developed in the accompanying manifesto. This companion contains a portable charter, twelve campaign lines, and a practical bridge to the existing pillars and participation missions.

## Principles charter

We choose a civilization with more intelligence, more agency, and more life. We pursue artificial general intelligence with urgency and want more people to share in what it makes possible. AGI is the opening act.

**1. Civilization is unfinished.** Present limits deserve investigation. Longer lives, deeper understanding, unfamiliar forms of creativity, and life beyond Earth are ambitions worth pursuing. Build toward them. Make room for people to choose different futures within a civilization capable of offering more.

**2. Pursue general intelligence.** Develop machines that can learn, reason, and act across unfamiliar tasks with broad competence. This objective does not depend on proving consciousness or predicting superintelligence. Support multiple research paths; demand demonstrations; pledge allegiance to no laboratory, architecture, benchmark, or arrival date.

**3. Improve the machinery of discovery.** Investigate whether AI can accelerate science, engineering, and the development of better AI. Treat that possibility as a research hypothesis with enormous stakes and unresolved limits. Build tools, run experiments, and distinguish an impressive output from a verified discovery.

**4. Intelligence should enlarge agency.** Give people capabilities, choices, and time. Defend privacy, consent, inquiry, and the freedom to refuse unwanted automation. Examine future synthetic moral status seriously without declaring today's systems conscious. Ask whose choices expand, who gains power, and who can meaningfully say no.

**5. Abundance must be built.** Intelligence needs energy, chips, infrastructure, robots, laboratories, factories, and institutions that can deliver. Turn capability into useful goods and services. Treat affordability and distribution as work to accomplish. A breakthrough behind an unaffordable subscription has unfinished business.

**6. Open by default.** Favor open research and weights, affordable access, interoperability, competition, and independent experimentation. Consider narrow restrictions when credible evidence shows severe risk; require reasons, scope, and review. Resist rules that convert the current winners into permanent gatekeepers. Make participation possible beyond permission from an incumbent.

**7. Count the costs of delay.** Include disease, deprivation, drudgery, and discoveries forgone when judging technological choices. Compare action, delay, and targeted alternatives honestly. Uncertainty belongs on every side of the ledger. Do not manufacture numerical certainty to sell urgency or to sell paralysis.

**8. Make progress resilient.** Build security, controllability, evaluation, and correction into the work. Act on credible warning signs before harm occurs; make interventions proportionate and reviewable. Challenge indefinite vetoes and impossible demands for certainty. The future has entered peer review. Please attach a prototype.

**9. Conviction must remain corrigible.** Publish evidence, acknowledge failure, invite criticism, and revise beliefs. Keep membership independent of loyalty to a founder, vendor, forecast, or technical fashion. Strong convictions. Updateable weights. Anyone claiming the movement has become infallible has volunteered to be its next debugging exercise.

**10. Culture helps construct the future.** Make ambitions understandable through art, humor, and memes. Credit creators; label imagined capabilities; turn attention into things people can build and share. Troll defeatism with imagination and receipts. Include ourselves among the legitimate targets. Maximum ambition needs a functioning sense of humor.

AI Maxxi welcomes builders, researchers, artists, critics, and curious newcomers. Participation requires no purchase. Token ownership confers no philosophical authority. Make something worth carrying forward.

## From principles to participation

The six existing pillars name areas of work. The principles explain why that work matters and how we intend to pursue it. The existing **Make the future tangible** mission gives contributors two ways to begin: **Build** for working artifacts with demonstrations, and **Imagine** for clearly labeled visions and cultural work.

| Pillar or mission track | Principles | Concrete contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum compute | 5, 6, 8 | Publish a reproducible guide to running a useful model on accessible hardware, including cost, limitations, and security assumptions. |
| Maximum intelligence | 2, 3, 6, 9 | Release a small evaluation or research tool with reproducible examples, failure cases, and a clear statement of what it measures. |
| Maximum agents | 3, 4, 8 | Demonstrate an agent that removes a tedious task while preserving approval, a usable stop control, and a record of its actions. |
| Maximum robots | 1, 4, 5, 8 | Show a bounded physical-world prototype or simulator; identify the environment, limits, and controls required before physical deployment. |
| Maximum energy | 1, 5, 7 | Build a transparent model or visual explanation of an energy constraint, citing inputs and distinguishing present capacity from a proposed future. |
| Maximum memes | 1, 9, 10 | Make an original poster, meme, or strange little film that turns one principle into a future people can picture. Credit sources and creators. |
| Build | 3, 4, 8, 9 | Submit a working artifact and demonstration. Explain what works, what remains incomplete, and how another person can check it. A mockup alone belongs in Imagine. |
| Imagine | 1, 5, 10 | Submit an original image, meme, film, or attributed remix. Label imagined capabilities as imagined; include source material, creator credit, and permission to feature. |

Choose the track that matches the work. Share through the existing editable X composer or public GitHub submission form. GitHub requires sign-in; submissions are public, so remove private information. Opening a destination does not submit anything. Neither track requires a token, wallet, or purchase, and neither promises a reward.

This document introduces no new mission schedule, staffing commitment, selection promise, or funding program. The live mission page remains the source for its current dates and submission instructions.
